Squat Kinematics, 2D/3D Pose Fusion & Joint Torque Lab Questions

Explore the sample trials in the Post-Recording Analysis Studio and review the biomechanical concepts below. Click each question to reveal the engineering explanation.

1

Why does computing joint angles directly from normalized [0, 1] video coordinates fail on portrait smartphones?

Consider the aspect ratio H / W of a vertical 9:16 smartphone video (360 × 640) during trunk lean and knee flexion.

Biomechanical & Computer Vision Explanation: MediaPipe normalizes 2D landmark coordinates (xi, yi) to [0, 1] by dividing horizontal pixel positions by image width W and vertical pixel positions by image height H. In a portrait smartphone video (W = 360, H = 640), one unit of normalized Δx represents 360 px while one unit of normalized Δy represents 640 px. Passing raw normalized differences into atan2(Δx, Δy) stretches horizontal displacements by H / W = 640 / 360 = 1.78×! Consequently, a healthy 25° athletic trunk lean is artificially inflated to arctan(1.78 · tan 25°) ≈ 40°, causing false posture errors on every repetition. Scaling coordinates to true pixel space (Xi = xiW, Yi = yiH) restores isotropic Euclidean geometry across any camera orientation.
2

How does our 2D Pixel + 3D World Landmark fusion overcome planar foreshortening in frontal and oblique camera views?

Compare Sample 1 (Cam0 Oblique) and Sample 1B (Cam1 Frontal) against the OpenSim Squat.mot ground truth.

Biomechanical & Computer Vision Explanation: When a camera views a squatter from the front (Cam1), the femur and tibia flex primarily along the camera's optical depth axis (Z). Projecting the 3D thigh and shank vectors onto the 2D image plane (X, Y) foreshortens their apparent angle, causing pure 2D knee flexion to read only 69.9°–97.3° when true OpenSim 3D knee flexion is 123.6°. Conversely, in oblique/sagittal views (Cam0), averaging the 2D pixel angles of the near and far legs cancels first-order perspective projection error. By computing the foreshortening deficit (k3D − k2D) and smoothly blending toward MediaPipe's metric 3D poseWorldLandmarks when 2D foreshortening is detected, our engine achieves 121.4° on Cam0 and 121.6° on Cam1 (<2.2° error across both views).
3

What are the biomechanical consequences of excessive forward trunk lean (>40°) vs. moderate athletic trunk lean (20°–35°)?

Analyze lumbar shear forces and the shifting moment arms at the hip and knee joints.

Biomechanical Mechanism: During a natural deep squat, moderate forward trunk inclination (20°–35°, as seen in the OpenSim ground truth where pelvis tilt + lumbar flexion reaches 24.4° at bottom and 35.5° during ascent) keeps the combined center of mass (COM) balanced over the mid-foot while recruiting the hip extensors (gluteus maximus and hamstrings). However, when trunk lean exceeds 40° (a "good-morning squat" compensation, often caused by weak quadriceps or restricted ankle dorsiflexion), the horizontal moment arm from the L4/L5 intervertebral disc to the upper-body COM increases dramatically as Ltorso sin(θtrunk). This magnifies compressive and anterior shear loads on the lumbar spine.
4

Why must a squat state machine require simultaneous knee AND hip flexion rather than hip flexion alone?

Examine what happens when a subject bends at the waist to pick up weights or pivots between sets.

Biomechanical State-Machine Design: A pure hip hinge (bending forward at the waist with straight legs, such as at t = 3.33 s in Cam0) produces large hip flexion (>35°) while knee flexion remains near zero (6.7°). If the descent state trigger only checks hipFlexion > 30°, any waist bend or torso rotation falsely enters the 'down' squat state. By requiring coupled multi-joint descent (θknee > 35° AND θhip > 30°) along with a minimum bottom depth (max θknee ≥ 45°) and minimum excursion duration (≥ 0.35 s), the state machine cleanly distinguishes true squats from hip hinges and pivot turns.
